- Focus mode (after 1.0): work through the due thoughts one at a time as a card deck, with "Tomorrow" and "Next thought" buttons. - Timeline mode (after 1.0): one continuous scroll through every thought ever written, newest first by creation date and grouped by day or month, so you can read the stream of thoughts as it happened. It ignores due dates (archived thoughts are an open question). It has to load in pages (`fetchLimit` plus `fetchOffset` or a date cursor), never as one `@Query` array, because of the "Assume hundreds of thousands of thoughts" rule in CLAUDE.md.
- Kindle highlights import (after 1.0): import the `My Clippings.txt` file a Kindle keeps in its `documents` folder (title and author, location and date, highlight, `==========` separator), parsed on device and opened from Files or the share sheet. It also catches sideloaded docs and highlights past the publisher's export cap. Never scrape `read.amazon.com/notebook`: it breaks the "only network calls are to our API" rule and is fragile. Open questions: one thought per highlight or per book; dedupe on re-import (the file only grows, so it needs a stable key such as a hash of book, location and text); tagging (`#kindle`, the book title); and whether it goes through `ThoughtStore.importThoughts` or `InboxImporter`. Files can hold thousands of entries. Maybe later: the Kindle app's "Export notes" email (HTML/CSV).
- Thought "power ups" (maybe later): mark a single thought to change how it looks or behaves, so a favorite stands out. First idea, prototyped 2026-10-09 and then dropped: a holographic trading-card foil. It used a rainbow gradient and glare that move with the phone's tilt (CoreMotion device motion, no permission prompt) and a slight 3D lean on the open thought. The toggle was "Power up" in the thought's ⋯ menu, and the marks were kept in UserDefaults. Lessons: the look needs real tuning per theme. `.colorDodge` washes out on light paper, while `.multiply` shows but was too loud on Ink. Stop motion updates when nothing is on screen (scene phase plus a watchdog, not only onAppear/onDisappear counts). Open questions: which other power ups (behavior as well as looks); whether the mark belongs in the model, and so in backups (a schema change after launch); and whether it's free or a paid extra.
- Cross-device sync (maybe later; Apple and non-Apple devices, so not iCloud): an optional paid subscription (around $2.99/month), off by default. Model it on Obsidian Sync, but sync records rather than files: our own server stores only end-to-end encrypted records (the key never leaves the user's devices, like the export link), images as content-addressed blobs uploaded once, and devices pair by scanning a QR code that carries the key, so there are no accounts. Merges use the per-group change clocks and tombstones that are already in the model (and the max-merged view counters); text conflicts get a three-way merge against the last synced version, or a conflict copy. Scheduling could instead sync as a log of actions (viewed, reviewed, snoozed) replayed through the pure scheduler. With sync off the app makes no sync network calls, but the privacy manifests, privacy policy and App Store privacy answers still change when it ships. Plan for hundreds of thousands of thoughts: batched, resumable first sync. Open questions: how an "erase everything" propagates (deleteAll currently clears tombstones), tombstone pruning, and the subscription's terms-of-use requirements.
- Purchasable themes (maybe later): six themes ship free in 1.0, so paid ones are only an idea. Open questions: one-off per theme or a single "all themes" unlock; alternate App Icons shipped with a theme; and the StoreKit privacy checks (Apple's network call, not ours, but check the privacy manifests, privacy policy and App Store privacy answers anyway). Lessons for new themes: Iowan Old Style has no Black weight on iOS; swipe-action labels are always white, so each theme needs its own swipe tints; `preferredColorScheme(nil)` doesn't reliably undo a forced scheme, so dark-only themes use window overrides.
