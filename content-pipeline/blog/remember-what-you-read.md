# How to remember what you read

**Status:** outline

- **Slug:** `how-to-remember-what-you-read`
- **Title:** How to remember what you read: 5 habits that actually work
- **Description:** Most of what we read is gone in a week. Here's why, and five simple habits, backed by memory research, that help the good parts stick.
- **Search phrases:** how to remember what you read, retain what you read, remember books, stop forgetting what you read
- **Length:** about 1,200–1,500 words, short sentences (grade 7), one heading per habit so search results can show them as a list.

## Outline

**1. Hook.** Think of the last book you finished. Can you name three ideas from it? Most people can't, and that's normal. It isn't a sign of a bad memory.

**2. Why we forget.** Ebbinghaus tested his own memory in the 1880s and found most forgetting happens in the first day or two, then slows down. Murre & Dros (2015) repeated the experiment and got a similar curve. The point: if you never come back to an idea, it's likely gone.

**3. What feels like it works but doesn't.** Dunlosky et al. (2013) rated rereading and highlighting as low value. They make the text feel familiar, and familiar feels like knowing.

**4. Five habits.** One research point and one concrete action each.

1. **Put it in your own words, right away.** One idea per note, not a summary of the whole book.
2. **Test yourself instead of rereading.** Roediger & Karpicke (2006): students who practiced recalling a passage remembered more a week later than students who reread it, even though rereading felt better at the time. Write the idea as a question, close the book, answer it.
3. **Space it out.** Come back after a few days, then a few weeks. The spacing effect is one of the most replicated findings in memory research (Cepeda et al.). Simple version: look at it again in a week.
4. **Connect it.** To your own life, an earlier book, or a theme you care about. Ideas with more connections are easier to recall.
5. **Use it.** Tell someone, act on it, or argue with it.

**5. A system you can start tonight.** No more than three ideas per book. Each on its own card (paper is fine) with a question on the front. Put it where you'll see it next week, and keep the ones worth coming back to. This keeps the post useful for someone who never buys the app.

**6. The app, lightly.** One short paragraph. Thought Reps does habits 1–3: each idea is a thought, it comes back after 7 days, and a blurred block hides the answer until you tap it, which is self-testing. ## Leave out

- **The "learning pyramid"** ("10% of what we read, 90% of what we teach"). It's a myth with no study behind it.
- **Exact numbers like "70% forgotten in 24 hours."** Ebbinghaus measured how much faster he relearned a list, not how much he forgot. Describe the shape of the curve instead.
- **Longhand vs. laptop notes** (Mueller & Oppenheimer 2014). Later attempts to repeat it got weaker results.
